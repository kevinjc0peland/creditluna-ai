Numbered notes: questions, not conclusions

1 · Seller, buyer, date. Does the buyer named in the first sale become the seller in the next one? Who claims the right to collect now?
2 · Portfolio and account schedule. The broad EX-42 label alone is not an account identifier. Is the referenced schedule attached, and do the name, last four and file reference connect to your own records?
3 · Warranty. What does the seller promise or disclaim about accuracy? An “as is” type clause does not establish that the consumer's balance is wrong.
4 · Signatures. Who signed for each party, in what capacity and on which date? A visible signature alone is not a ruling on the validity of a transfer.
5 · Next link and redactions. Does Schedule B identify the same account? Redacting other people's rows is common for privacy; what remains should still help answer, “Does this connect to my account?” A blacked-out page is not inherently fake.
Ask, don't assume. If a schedule is missing or identifiers conflict, describe the precise gap. Neither a full unredacted public document nor any particular packet is a universal federal validation requirement. A lawyer can assess local court rules.
