How to make your first credit report dispute: simple, specific and documented

Your first credit-report dispute does not need a legal argument. Say what the report shows, why it is wrong, and what records support you. This is separate from a debt-validation request to a collector. CFPB dispute guide

Read it yourself: CFPB's credit-report dispute steps · FCRA dispute rule, 15 U.S.C. § 1681i · CFPB's free-report guide · CFPB complaint notice.

Key takeaways

  • Get the report from each bureau that has the item, and mark the exact entry you dispute. CFPB free reports · CFPB dispute steps
  • Give one clear, factual reason for each item; send copies of useful proof, not originals. CFPB
  • Keep dated copies of what you sent, proof it was received, and each reply. This helps if you need to follow up, complain, or speak with a lawyer. CFPB dispute steps · CFPB follow-up rights

Get the report and circle the exact problem

The CFPB says you can get a free report from each of the three nationwide bureaus once a week at AnnualCreditReport.com. See which reports contain the item. CFPB

Print the page and circle or highlight the part you believe is inaccurate or incomplete. For example, “This balance does not show my payment on [date].” Say only what you know. Include the marked page if mailing the dispute. CFPB

Write a short letter in your own words

Give your contact details, report confirmation number if any, and the item you want checked. Write one specific reason per item and request a correction or removal. Include copies, not originals, of useful records. Never claim an account is not yours unless true. CFPB mail-dispute checklist

Dispute with each bureau whose report has the error, and consider a separate dispute with the information provider. For mail, certified delivery and a return receipt can document receipt. Check current routes: Equifax, Experian, TransUnion. CFPB

Keep a small file of everything

Keep the report, letter, evidence, mailing or online confirmation, delivery proof, and every response. Note dates sent, received, and answered. Scan or photograph paper records; save PDFs or photos on a protected phone or computer. If a reply is late or missing, your file shows what happened and when. CFPB · FTC

Protect the device with a password and, when possible, two-factor authentication. When sharing records outside the bureau/provider dispute, black out SSN and account numbers the recipient does not need. Leave details the bureau needs to investigate visible. FTC · CFPB

Know what the law says about the answer

Under 15 U.S.C. § 1681i(a)(1)(A), the bureau generally must reinvestigate within 30 days of receiving the dispute. Subparagraph (B) permits up to 15 extra days if you submit relevant information during those 30 days; (C) bars that extension if the item was already found inaccurate or incomplete or unverifiable. The CFPB also describes a 45-day case following a free annual report.

Section 1681i(a)(6)(A) says the company must provide written notice of results within five business days after completing the reinvestigation, by mail or, if you authorized it, another available means. Paragraph (6)(B) describes what comes with it, including a statement that the reinvestigation is done and a report based on the revised file. There is an expedited-deletion exception in (a)(8); do not assume every fast deletion follows the usual notice path.

Under § 1681i(a)(5)(A), if an item is found inaccurate or incomplete or cannot be verified after reinvestigation, the company must promptly delete or modify it as appropriate. But § 1681i(a)(3) permits it to end a reinvestigation it reasonably finds frivolous or irrelevant, including for too little information, with notice as the statute requires. This is why plain facts and a circled report help. Accurate negative information can lawfully remain; a dispute promises no deletion or score change. CFPB reporting guide

If your debt was sold: check who is collecting and where

Creditors may sell or refer debts. Ask who owns the debt now and how the collector is connected to your account. A collector's validation notice names the current creditor; under § 1692g, a timely written request seeks the original creditor's name and address if different. You may ask for the chain of ownership, but federal law does not guarantee a full chain on request. If sued, the collector must prove its claim to the court. If your state licenses collectors, ask for a license number and check with your state attorney general or financial regulator. Requirements differ; California's regulator points to a license search, while the Colorado attorney general describes its collection-agency licensing rules. An out-of-state address alone does not settle whether collection is lawful.

For suits other than actions to enforce an interest in real property, 15 U.S.C. § 1692i(a)(2) generally requires a collector to sue in a district “in which such consumer signed the contract sued upon” or “in which such consumer resides at the commencement of the action.” This means the appropriate judicial district or similar legal entity, not just a state. An out-of-state collector is not, by itself, unusual or proof of improper venue; counsel can advise. Colorado AG If served a summons, do not ignore it. Check the response deadline in the papers; missing it can lead to a default judgment. CFPB

If the response arrives, or does not

Compare the result and updated report with your saved copy. If still wrong or no answer arrives, note exactly what remains, check the dates and dispute status, and keep your reply for a follow-up. The CFPB explains options, including a statement, complaint, and legal help. You may seek a lawyer anytime.

For a CFPB complaint about inaccurate or incomplete information against a reporting company, the CFPB complaint notice says to dispute with that company first, and not to submit while the dispute is pending unless more than 45 days have elapsed. Use your dated records to state what happened; read the notice before filing at consumerfinance.gov/complaint. A complaint is not a guaranteed correction.

Three common questions

Should I send a long legal letter? No special script is needed. Say exactly which item you question, why, and what records support you. CFPB

Does every item I dispute get removed? No. The law calls for correction or deletion as appropriate only if an item is found inaccurate or incomplete or cannot be verified after reinvestigation. Accurate negative information can remain. 15 U.S.C. § 1681i(a)(5) · CFPB

What if I never hear back? Check the delivery date, the statutory timing and any exception, and the bureau's dispute status. Keep your proof, consider a follow-up or CFPB complaint when eligible, and seek legal advice at any time. FCRA notice rule · CFPB follow-up guidance
